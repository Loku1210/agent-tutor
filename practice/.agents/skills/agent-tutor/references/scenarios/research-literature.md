# Scenario: Research and literature synthesis

Use this starter playbook when the learner wants to search and synthesize literature, summarize local papers or reading notes, or map open gaps and candidate research directions.

## Clarify first (1–2 questions max)

- What is the scope: topic keywords, time window, domain/model or population, and whether we are using local files or online search?
- What deliverable do they want first: a structured comparison table, a short state-of-the-field brief with open gaps, or a reusable search strategy and prompt?

## Reality and verification rules

Check what tools, local files, and network access are actually available in the current session, then follow the matching case:

- **Online search available:** use real sources only, include verifiable identifiers (title, year, journal/preprint, link or DOI), and separate observed data, correlational/in-silico findings, and causal claims.
- **Local papers or notes available, but online search unavailable:** synthesize the provided local materials first, state the exact file coverage upfront, and put search queries plus external verification items in a separate section for later online follow-up. Never fabricate external citations or DOIs.
- **Neither online search nor local papers or notes available:** state the limitation clearly upfront. Never fabricate citations, DOIs, or statistics; instead deliver a ready-to-run search query, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and a local synthesis template.

## Smallest workable deliverable

Deliver in the agreed format (a Markdown brief such as `lit-brief.md`, a chat draft, or a prompt only) containing: search scope, concise evidence summary or comparison table, open gaps / limitations, candidate next questions, and items still needing manual verification. Before writing a file, check whether the target filename already exists; if it is taken, use a clearly stated new name or ask before replacing it. If file writing is unavailable or not requested, deliver the draft in chat.

## Concrete learner check

Ask the learner to pick 1–2 cited papers or local notes and verify that the title, key finding, and claim strength match the source without overstatement.

## Optional next step

If the learner plans to process many papers or add an independent fact-check pass, introduce task splitting and verification gates with [choosing how to work](../choosing-how-to-work.md).
